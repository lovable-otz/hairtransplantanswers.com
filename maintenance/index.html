Hair transplant aftercare: how to protect your results

The first two weeks matter most. Sleep elevated, follow your wash routine exactly, avoid touching the grafts, and skip strenuous exercise until you're cleared. Do that, and your new follicles settle in for a natural, lasting result. Here's the full timeline, plus the do's and don'ts.

Do's & don'ts

What should I do - and avoid - while healing?

Do

  • Sleep with your head elevated for the first few nights
  • Follow the wash routine and product exactly
  • Take prescribed medication and attend follow-ups
  • Protect your scalp from direct sun

Don't

  • Touch, scratch, or pick at grafts or scabs
  • Do strenuous exercise for about two weeks
  • Wear tight hats until your clinic clears you
  • Smoke or drink alcohol during early healing

When and how should I wash my hair after a transplant?

Most clinics have you start gentle washing around day 2–3, using a mild product and a careful pour-and-pat technique rather than rubbing. The goal is to keep the area clean and help scabs flake away naturally without dislodging grafts. Always follow your surgeon's exact method and timing.

When can I exercise, drink alcohol, or return to normal?

Light activity often resumes within a few days, but strenuous exercise, heavy sweating, swimming, and saunas are usually paused for around two weeks. Alcohol and smoking are best avoided in early healing because they can impair recovery. Your clinic will give you a personalised timeline.

How do I protect my results long-term?

Transplanted follicles are permanent, but your original hair can keep thinning. Many surgeons recommend continuing medication (like minoxidil or finasteride) to protect non-transplanted hair, plus sun protection and gentle care. See what treatments actually work.
