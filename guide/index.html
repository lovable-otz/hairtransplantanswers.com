The complete guide to hair transplants

A hair transplant moves your own permanent follicles from a donor area to thinning or bald areas, where they keep growing for life. This independent guide covers how it works, the main methods (FUE, FUT, DHI), non-surgical options, who's a candidate, results, safety, and cost - everything you need before deciding.

What is a hair transplant, and how does it work?

A hair transplant is a minor surgical procedure that relocates healthy hair follicles from a donor area - usually the back and sides of the head - to thinning or bald areas. Because donor follicles are genetically resistant to balding, they continue to grow in their new location, giving a permanent, natural-looking result.

What is FUE (follicular unit extraction)?

FUE is the most common modern technique. The surgeon extracts follicular units one by one with a tiny punch, then implants them into the recipient area. It leaves no linear scar and has a faster recovery than older strip methods - which is why it dominates the market. Learn more in how the procedure works.

What hair-restoration methods are there?

There are two main surgical methods (FUE and FUT), a variation called DHI, and non-surgical treatments (PRP, stem-cell therapy, medication). Here's how the surgical options compare at a glance - see the full FUE vs FUT comparison.

MethodScarringRecoveryBest for
FUETiny dot scarsFasterMost patients, shorter styles
FUTOne linear scarSlowerLarge sessions, lower per-graft cost
DHIMinimalSimilar to FUEPrecise, dense packing

Am I a good candidate for a hair transplant?

The best candidates have stable hair loss, a healthy donor area with good density, and realistic expectations. Age, the cause of your loss, and how much donor hair you have all matter. A consultation confirms candidacy - start with the signs and candidacy checklist.

Are the results permanent, and when will I see them?

Yes - transplanted follicles typically grow for life. After surgery the new hairs shed within a few weeks (this is normal), then regrow from around month three. You'll see meaningful density by 6–9 months and final results near 12 months.

Is a hair transplant safe? What are the risks?

Performed by a qualified surgeon, a hair transplant is a low-risk outpatient procedure. Uncommon risks include infection, poor growth, or an unnatural result from bad technique. Choosing an experienced, accredited clinic is the single biggest safeguard - see how to choose a clinic and what can go wrong.

Do non-surgical options (PRP, stem cell) work?

PRP (platelet-rich plasma) and stem-cell therapies can support existing hair and are sometimes used alongside surgery, but they don't regrow a fully bald area on their own. Medications like minoxidil and finasteride slow loss but must be continued. See DIY vs professional for what actually works.
