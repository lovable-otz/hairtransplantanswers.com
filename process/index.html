How it works

How does a hair transplant work?

A hair transplant is a same-day outpatient procedure. A surgeon extracts healthy follicles from your donor area, prepares them, and implants them into thinning areas - all under local anaesthetic. It usually takes 4–8 hours, and new hair grows in over the following 6–12 months. Here's every step.

Step by step

What are the steps of a hair transplant?

Five stages, from your first assessment to new growth.

1

Consultation & planning

A surgeon assesses your hair-loss pattern and donor density, agrees a realistic graft count, and designs a natural hairline with you.

2

Donor harvesting

Healthy follicles are removed from the donor area - individually with FUE, or as a strip with FUT. The area is numbed with local anaesthetic first.

3

Graft preparation

The team inspects and sorts the grafts under magnification, keeping them viable while the recipient sites are prepared.

4

Implantation

Each graft is placed at the correct angle, depth, and direction to match your natural growth pattern and density.

5

Recovery & growth

You go home the same day. Transplanted hairs shed, then regrow - with new density building over the next 6–12 months.

How does the FUE procedure work?

With FUE (follicular unit extraction), the surgeon uses a tiny punch to remove individual follicular units from the donor area, then implants them one by one. There's no linear scar and recovery is quicker than older strip methods. It's the most common technique today - compare it with FUT on the types & options page.

How long does a hair transplant take, and is it painful?

Most procedures take 4–8 hours depending on the number of grafts. Because it's done under local anaesthetic, you're awake but shouldn't feel pain during it - just some pressure. Mild soreness, tightness, or swelling for a few days afterward is normal and manageable with simple pain relief.

How does Bosley hair restoration work?

Bosley is a well-known US provider that performs both FUE and FUT hair transplants. The underlying process is the same as described above: donor harvesting, graft preparation, and implantation under local anaesthetic, followed by the standard recovery timeline.
