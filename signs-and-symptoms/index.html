Signs of hair loss: are you a candidate for a transplant?

The earliest signs of pattern hair loss are a receding hairline, a thinning crown, a widening part, and more shedding than usual. Spotting them early gives you the most options. This guide covers the warning signs, what the Norwood and Ludwig stages mean, and how to tell if a transplant could be right for you.

Early warning signs

What are the early signs of hair loss?

Six things to watch for - noticing them early gives you the most options.

A receding hairline

The hairline moves back, especially at the temples, forming an "M" shape.

A thinning crown

Hair thins or a bald spot appears at the top-back of the head.

A widening part

Your parting looks wider than before, with more scalp visible.

More shedding

Noticeably more hair on your pillow, in the shower, or on your brush.

Visible scalp in light

Your scalp shows through more, particularly under bright or overhead light.

Family history

Pattern hair loss is largely genetic, so relatives with it raise your likelihood.

Know your stage

What do hair-loss patterns (Norwood & Ludwig) mean?

The Norwood scale (men) and Ludwig scale (women) describe how far loss has progressed - and what it usually means for treatment.

StageWhat it looks likeTypical approach
Norwood I–IIMinor hairline recessionMonitor; medication may help
Norwood IIIDeeper temple recessionMedication ± transplant
Norwood IV–VHairline and crown thinningTransplant often suitable
Norwood VI–VIIExtensive loss, limited donorTransplant if donor allows
Ludwig I–III (women)Diffuse thinning, part wideningAssess cause; medication ± transplant

Am I a good candidate for a hair transplant?

The best candidates have hair loss that has stabilised, defined thinning or bald areas, and a healthy donor area with good density. Realistic expectations matter too. A consultation assesses your donor supply and pattern to confirm whether a transplant will give a natural, lasting result.

What causes hair loss, and does the cause affect treatment?

Most pattern hair loss is genetic and hormonal (androgenetic alopecia). Other causes - stress, illness, diet, or medical conditions - can cause temporary shedding that often recovers. The cause matters: a transplant treats permanent pattern loss, not temporary shedding, so diagnosis comes first.

When should I see a hair-restoration specialist?

See a specialist as soon as you notice ongoing thinning or recession - earlier gives you more options, including medication to protect existing hair. A specialist can confirm the cause, your Norwood/Ludwig stage, and whether a transplant is worthwhile. We can match you with vetted clinics for an assessment.
